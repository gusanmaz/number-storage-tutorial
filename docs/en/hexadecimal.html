Hexadecimal

Hex is a human-readable shorthand for binary. Each hex digit carries exactly 4 bits, which is why programmers write memory addresses, colors and raw data in hex.

⏱ ~25 minPrerequisite: Binary
By the end of this chapter you will
  • recognise the hex digits 0–F and their 4-bit patterns;
  • convert binary ↔ hex just by grouping, without any arithmetic;
  • convert hex to decimal and decimal to hex;
  • read real hex values such as color codes, MAC addresses and memory addresses.

Why hex?

Let’s write the same 32-bit value three ways:

Binary1101 1110 1010 1101 1011 1110 1110 111132 characters, hard to read
Decimal3735928559Short, but the bits are invisible
HexDEAD BEEF8 characters, each digit = 4 bits

The magic of hex: 16 = 2⁴. So every hex digit corresponds to exactly 4 bits. A group of 4 bits is called a nibble. Two nibbles make a byte, so 1 byte = 2 hex digits. Decimal has no such clean relationship: 255 and 256 both have three digits, but one fits in 8 bits and the other doesn’t.

1 hex digit = 4 bits   ·   2 hex digits = 8 bits = 1 byte

Sixteen digits

We need sixteen different digits but only have 0–9. Letters fill in the missing six:

Memory trick

A = 10 (first letter = first two-digit number), F = 15 (the largest, all bits 1: 1111). The rest follow in order. When reading 4 bits, remember the weights 8 4 2 1. For example 1011 = 8 + 2 + 1 = 11 = B.

Binary ↔ hex: just group

To go from binary to hex, group the bits in fours starting from the right. If the leftmost group is short, pad it with zeros (leading zeros don’t change the value). Then turn each group into a single hex digit. Hex to binary is the reverse: expand each hex digit into 4 bits.

Nibble grouper

Type a binary or hex value. Faded zeros are padding added to complete a group.

Hex → decimal

The place-value formula still applies, just with base 16. Weights from right to left: 1, 16, 256, 4096…

2A16

2 × 16 + A × 1
= 2 × 16 + 10 × 1
= 32 + 10 = 42

1F416

1 × 256 + F × 16 + 4 × 1
= 256 + 240 + 4
= 500

Decimal → hex

Two ways:

  1. Divide by 16 repeatedly (the algorithm from chapter 1). Remainders 10–15 become letters.
  2. Go via binary: often quicker for small numbers.
500 ÷ 16 = 31   remainder  4
 31 ÷ 16 =  1   remainder 15 → F
  1 ÷ 16 =  0   remainder  1
                           ↑ read bottom to top → 1F4₁₆

Hex in the wild

Common mistake

0x10 is not ten, it is sixteen. To avoid reading hex as decimal, it is always prefixed with 0x (in code) or # (in colors), or marked with a subscript 16. Saying “one-zero hex” is safer than saying “ten”.

Summary

  • Hex has 16 digits: 0–9 and A(10)–F(15).
  • 16 = 2⁴ → 1 hex digit = 4 bits (a nibble), 2 hex digits = 1 byte.
  • Binary → hex: group in 4s from the right. Hex → binary: expand each digit to 4 bits.
  • Hex → decimal: place values 1, 16, 256, 4096… Decimal → hex: divide by 16.
  • Code uses the 0x prefix, colors use #.

What is 3C₁₆ in decimal?

  • 60
  • 48
  • 36
  • 312
3 × 16 + C(12) = 48 + 12 = 60.

How is 1111 0011₂ written in hex?

  • F11
  • E3
  • F3
  • FC
1111 = F, 0011 = 3 → F3.

How many bits does a 6-digit hex value (e.g. #FF8800) carry?

  • 6
  • 24
  • 48
  • 12
6 × 4 = 24 bits = 3 bytes. That is why color codes are 24-bit.

In hex, what comes after FF?

  • FG
  • G0
  • FF1
  • 100
Both digits are at the largest digit (F), so both carry: FF + 1 = 100₁₆ = 256.

How many bytes is the MAC address 00:1A:2B:3C:4D:5E?

  • 12
  • 6
  • 3
  • 48
Each pair of hex digits between colons is one byte: 6 groups = 6 bytes = 48 bits.
